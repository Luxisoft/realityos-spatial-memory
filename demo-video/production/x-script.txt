What if a photo could take you straight to the information you are allowed to see? This is Reality O S.

I'm José Marín, a solo builder in Build for Your World. Reality O S connects visual search, authorized records, and shared context through Android and a Meta glasses preview.

First, I select a local Police account. Its fictional case records distinguish active arrest warrants from missing-person search orders. Each account sees only information shared within its workspace.

I attach a photo and ask the chat to search for people with outstanding arrest warrants. The response brings five warrant-related records into view. I open the record labeled Esteban Pardo to inspect its details and original portrait. The app also flags uncertainty about the photo match, so these results require review. Warrant status comes from stored case information, never from assumptions about someone's appearance.

Next, Meta Social Media demonstrates a social network with synthetic profiles. This local example does not connect to Facebook live.

Here is the simulated Meta Display preview inside the app. I choose person search and capture a reference portrait with the phone camera. A profile candidate appears. Opening it brings the authorized profile into Android, where I can inspect the information shared with me. This demonstrates the glasses interaction through a working phone preview, rather than physical glasses footage.

Meta models support the broader system. Muse Spark interprets visual and contextual requests. SAM supports object segmentation, and Muse Voice Transcribe handles speech input. Face comparison uses a separate component. Workspace permissions and consent requirements remain part of the application.

During the hackathon, I added video, glasses workflows, profile search, and linked records. The same foundation could extend to care workflows and everyday memory.
